Work in Progress

Demand Elasticity in the Government Bond Market

Abstract

This paper studies how the elasticity of demand for government bonds affects sovereign financing. Using quarterly data on Korea's domestic government bond market from 2009:Q4 to 2022:Q4, I estimate the bond demand of a representative investor, instrumenting the yield with the general government deficit as a supply shifter. The estimated demand curve is downward sloping and inelastic: the elasticity of holdings with respect to the yield is 0.22. Motivated by these findings, I incorporate downward-sloping bond demand into a quantitative sovereign default model with long-term debt and discipline the demand elasticity with the observed gap between the hedged Treasury bond spread and the CDS spread. At the calibrated elasticity, the inelasticity wedge accounts for roughly forty percent of the Korean sovereign spread, and its share grows with the stock of debt. These results highlight the importance of investor demand elasticity for sovereign borrowing costs and debt dynamics.
